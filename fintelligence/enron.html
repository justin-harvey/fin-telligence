The Enron reporting gap
what was reported, versus what the rows support.

Enron's FY2000 annual report is the case the whole thesis is haunted by: numbers that were internally consistent on paper and disconnected from reality. Fin-Telligence runs the same verified pipeline over it, computing the reported figure and the underlying figure in one guarded query — so the distance between them is a grounded, hash-chained, tamper-evident result, not an assertion.

Two mechanics of the gap

Revenue: gross vs. net

Trades booked at full notional turned $40bn into $100bn.

Reported revenue (gross) real 10-K $100,789m
Merchant margin actually earned (net) modelled $1,953m
Revenue was booked on a gross basis — the full notional of each energy trade counted as revenue. The net merchant margin is ~1.9% of the headline.

Debt: reported vs. true

Billions parked off the balance sheet in special-purpose entities.

Reported debt (on balance sheet) real 10-K $10,229m
True debt incl. off-balance-sheet SPEs illustrative $21,729m
Debt held in the SPEs (JEDI, Chewco, LJM1/2, Raptor, Whitewing) never touched the reported balance sheet. Consolidating them more than doubles the leverage.

What is real, and what is synthetic

The aggregates are real. Total revenues, net income, and reported debt reconcile to Enron's actual reported figures, transcribed verbatim from the filing and stored with the citation on every row. The transaction-level rows are synthetic — there is no public Enron general ledger — and the off-balance-sheet SPE amounts are illustrative of the mechanism, not a claimed exact historical total. For a tool about traceable figures, fabricated data labelled as fabricated is fine; fabricated data presented as real is the exact failure this project exists to prevent. Nothing here is a compliance claim.
