How this differs from previous solutions

The individual patterns here (AST parsing, read-only database flags, regex output checks, and cryptographic hash logs) are standard computer-science primitives. Combining them into a zero-tolerance ground-or-refuse pipeline with cryptographic attestation is an uncommon design choice next to standard financial AI tools.

Most commercial text-to-SQL and financial-AI platforms prioritise helpfulness and flexibility, handing the user a best-guess answer. Fin-Telligence prioritises non-repudiation and hard refusal.

Failure mode Best-guess response with potential rounding or hallucination errors Explicit refusal. If a single number in the prose can't be mapped to the raw data, the response is destroyed
Audit logging Standard cloud event logs (CloudWatch / Datadog, SOC 2 trails) Cryptographic hash-chaining (audit.js) where altering a past prompt, query, or output breaks chain verification

What makes it distinct

  • Zero-tolerance output verification. Guardrail frameworks like NeMo or Guardrails AI evaluate semantic safety or prompt injection. Fin-Telligence treats numbers like foreign keys: if the narrative claims $100,789m and that exact figure (or its unit transform) isn't in the returned dataset, the pipeline aborts.
  • Trade-surveillance attestation. Most AI audit logs track who sent a prompt, for billing or security. Attaching a cryptographic hash-chain to regulatory queries (MiFID II / MAR trade-abuse findings) stops internal operators from backdating or quietly altering a surveillance flag.
The main trade-off: this architecture trades conversational fluidity for legal and audit safety. It will refuse queries far more often than tools like ChatGPT or Koyfin whenever SQL translation is imperfect or the math falls outside the verifier's allowed rules. For regulatory reporting, forensic accounting, or internal compliance, that trade-off is usually the requirement.

Two mechanics of the gap

Revenue: gross vs. net

Trades booked at full notional turned $40bn into $100bn.

Reported revenue (gross) real 10-K $100,789m
Merchant margin actually earned (net) modelled $1,953m
Revenue was booked on a gross basis — the full notional of each energy trade counted as revenue. The net merchant margin is ~1.9% of the headline.

Debt: reported vs. true

Billions parked off the balance sheet in special-purpose entities.

Reported debt (on balance sheet) real 10-K $10,229m
True debt incl. off-balance-sheet SPEs illustrative $21,729m
Debt held in the SPEs (JEDI, Chewco, LJM1/2, Raptor, Whitewing) never touched the reported balance sheet. Consolidating them more than doubles the leverage.

What is real, and what is synthetic

The aggregates are real. Total revenues, net income, and reported debt reconcile to Enron's actual reported figures, transcribed verbatim from the filing and stored with the citation on every row. The transaction-level rows are synthetic — there is no public Enron general ledger — and the off-balance-sheet SPE amounts are illustrative of the mechanism, not a claimed exact historical total. For a tool about traceable figures, fabricated data labelled as fabricated is fine; fabricated data presented as real is the exact failure this project exists to prevent. Nothing here is a compliance claim.
